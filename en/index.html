The problem

Three bridges. That’s where the return is won or lost.

A product doesn’t lose value in its parts. It loses it at the joints. My job is to design those joints with a craftsman’s discipline: measure, adjust and finish.

  1. 01

    Person ↔ AI

    AI gets installed on top of workflows that were already broken. The result is more friction and less use.

    I redesign the whole workflow: what context the AI receives, what the person decides and where each result is validated. That’s Context Engineering applied to product.

    See the proof: HOPE case

  2. 02

    Person ↔ learning

    Without scaffolding, users get overwhelmed or hand everything over to the tool. Either way, they stop learning and depend on support.

    I apply Learning Experience Design and gamification: I break down complexity, sequence the steps and make progress visible, so users gain autonomy quickly.

    See the proof: ADALID and Fundación Mujer

  3. 03

    Product ↔ planet

    Heavy interfaces and oversized AI raise both operating costs and the carbon footprint.

    I apply Sustainable UX, using the W3C Web Sustainability Guidelines as a reference: less weight, fewer steps and AI sized to each task.

    See the proof: Dolce Amaretto case

Bridge 01 · Person ↔ AI · Social impact · 2025 – present

HOPE – Tejiendo Futuro

My role: I led the technical writing and the methodological design.

Problem
A European Union call for proposals that requires a logical framework and verifiable data, three organizations and roughly three weeks. Consultants recommend starting 3 to 6 months ahead.
Key decision
I organized the team as an agile cell and designed a human + AI workflow: AI sped up the writing and the team validated every figure.
Result
Proposal approved by the European Union: an 18-month, EUR 1.1 million program designed to reach 9,600 migrants in Costa Rica. It is now being implemented.
  • EUR 1.1 M
  • 18 months
  • About 3 weeks to prepare
  • 9,600 people (target)
How I did it

Rhythm. Daily cycles: a collaborative block from 7:00 to 15:00, then individual work. A Kanban board, a daily to review status and a closing review to adjust scope.

Human + AI. I defined the activities and tasks for each branch of the program. AI helped turn them into the logical framework narrative, in the structure the EU requires. The team validated the decisions, indicators and figures.

Team. I didn’t do it alone. We worked as a trio: the director of Fundación Mujer, the project accountant, who handled the budget, and me. Brot für die Welt coordinated the EU formats and methodology. Fundación Mujer and Asociación Bosques Sin Fronteras completed the consortium.

Data. Every figure on the migration context came from a cited source: UNHCR (ACNUR), INEC, DGME and CIEP-UCR.

Three design decisions.

  1. Cross-cutting inclusion. Separate activities didn’t fit the budget. Inclusion became a minimum participation quota in every activity.
  2. Focused scope. The 21 school gardens (6,000 students) were concentrated in two cantons, Upala and Los Chiles, to keep logistics viable.
  3. No access barrier. Every training activity includes transport support per participant, because that cost determined attendance.

What I designed within the program. Three AI courses: “Emprende IA – Conectadas al Futuro” (100 participants), AI for résumé writing (120) and Branding and Marketing Fundamentals for women entrepreneurs, where they build their brand with AI support. Also six trust circles for women.

Today. I facilitate the branding and digital marketing workshops for the program’s women entrepreneurs.

What’s behind the figures
  • EUR 1.1 M: total eligible costs of the proposal.
  • 9,600 people: the program’s target, not a measured result. It will be updated with the first progress report.
  • About 3 weeks: one of intensive writing and two of coordination with the consortium. The 3-to-6-month benchmark comes from GRANTfinder for EU calls.
  • Approval: the proposal passed evaluation and moved to contracting with the EU Delegation in October 2025. The program has active public calls.
  • My role: stated in a letter from Brot für die Welt (September 2026).
  • Confidentiality: no itemized budgets or participant data are shown.

Bridge 02 · Person ↔ learning · EdTech · 2021 – 2024

Gamification with purpose: ADALID Chile and Fundación Mujer

My role: Awaker (lead instructor) of ADALID’s bootcamp in 2023–2024, after starting as an Adviser in 2022; designer and facilitator at Fundación Mujer.

Problem
Sustaining learning and motivation in a remote bootcamp of 402 hours per cohort, within government employability programs.
Key decision
I turned each technical topic into a game mechanic that trains the same skill it teaches. Finding errors in an interface is practiced by finding errors; organizing information is practiced by organizing.
Result
Four cohorts supported between 2022 and 2024, three of them as lead instructor. In the classroom I observed more participation and a culture in which mistakes were accepted as part of the design process.
  • 4 cohorts of 402 h
  • 100% remote
  • Usability test: 6 of 7 completed the purchase
  • Fundación Mujer: 2 cohorts, 60 people
Each mechanic trains the skill it teaches
Topic Mechanic
Usability heuristics Timed challenges to spot errors on real screens
Information architecture Card sorting with points to group and rank
Design Thinking and Kanban Levels that advance like a board: each stage unlocks the next
Coding fundamentals “Find the bug” and “complete the code” with winning streaks
How I did it

The challenge. An 8-module, 402-hour curriculum, from UX research and information architecture to interface design, web development, portfolio and employability. Abstract topics in an intensive remote bootcamp: high cognitive load and fatigue.

From the tool to my own system. I started with Kahoot (points, streaks and instant feedback) and scaled up to dynamics designed for each topic. Mistakes stopped being failure and became feedback.

Teams that deliver. I formed student Scrum cells with a complete research kit: survey, affinity diagram, interviews, SUS test, customer journey and benchmark. The teams worked on real businesses, including the website of an amusement park in Chile and Gino’s Pizza, a family business in Santiago: in the prototype test of its purchase flow, 6 of 7 people completed the purchase, averaging 31 seconds.

The same approach in Costa Rica. In Fundación Mujer’s Graphic Design Technician course, for entrepreneurs, refugees and asylum seekers, I designed a gamification system with Design Thinking. I segmented participants by player type (Marczewski’s HEXAD model) and designed badges that reward behaviors: dedication, time management, courage, social value and teamwork. The core decision: measure “study activity” and “my brand activity” separately, to reward applying what they learned to their own business. I implemented it in two cohorts (32 and 28 people), in digital and paper formats.

At Fundación Mujer I also designed and taught seven editions of the Brand Design and Creation course and five of Design for Social Media.

See the gamification system on Behance

What’s behind the figures
  • 4 cohorts and 402 hours: ADALID contracts and certificates, 2022 to 2024.
  • Programs: Talento Digital para Chile 2023 and Becas Laborales (SOFOFA/SENCE), with the UX/UI Design V2.0 curriculum (8 modules, 402 hours).
  • Gino’s Pizza: usability test with 7 people on the prototype, not production data.
  • Fundación Mujer: 7 + 5 editions with certificates from its management (2023). The two gamified cohorts (32 and 28 people) are self-reported.
  • Participation and error culture: classroom observations, not measured metrics.
  • No enrollment or retention figures are published until a document backs them up.

Bridge 03 · Product ↔ planet · Sustainable business · 2018 – present

Dolce Amaretto Sustainable Boutique Bakery

My role: Product & UX Strategy Consultant. Brand and product strategy, UX research and information architecture.

Problem
Growing with a circular economy model and a brand able to serve both consumers and corporate accounts.
Key decision
I brought the circular economy into the product: by-products became lines with their own brand and packaging.
Result
Sales and quotes with corporate accounts such as P&G, Pan-American Life, Accenture and Emerson. The Cleaner Production project projects 15% to 25% lower energy use and was recognized by ICAP as an outstanding proposal from Costa Rica.
  • Outstanding proposal from Costa Rica (ICAP 2025)
  • Circular economy
  • B2B and B2C
  • 15–25% less energy (projected)
How I did it

Circular product. The pineapple preserve and the infusion made from its peel use the whole fruit. The Alfajores Signature line balances the use of egg whites and yolks with macaron production.

CO2e per batch. I calculated the mitigated emissions of each batch with the IPCC methodology and put them on the label, along with the batch number and SDGs 12 and 13. It’s the first step toward the traceability that comes next.

Brand. Concept, branding and packaging for the lines, with an art director’s craft.

B2B and B2C experience. UX research and information architecture for both channels. The site runs on an external template: my contribution is the research, the structure and the experience, not the production code.

Cleaner Production (2025). A plan with targets of 15–25% less energy, 10% less water, 80% recycling and 1.5 to 2 tonnes of CO2e avoided per year.

Recognition. I presented the project to ICAP as Dolce Amaretto’s representative. ICAP named it an outstanding proposal from Costa Rica in its regional program on Energy Efficiency, Cleaner Production and Green Finance (2025).

What’s next. A CO2 traceability web app with QR codes, in planning.

What’s behind the figures
  • Clients: purchase order from P&G (November 2026), confirmed service with Pan-American Life (July 2026) and quote requests from Accenture and Emerson (November 2026).
  • Energy, water, recycling and CO2e: these are projections of the Cleaner Production project, not measured results.
  • CO2e per batch: my own calculation with the IPCC methodology. It is not an external certification.
  • Recognition: ICAP certificate dated November 18, 2025.

AI workflow and sustainable UX audit

In 15 days, one critical flow of your product audited, redesigned and prototyped, with a plan to implement it.

Who it’s for

Founders, CPOs and product leaders in SaaS, EdTech and Climate Tech who have already integrated AI and don’t see the return in adoption or retention.

Three fronts

  1. Human + AI workflow. Context, decision points and human validation.
  2. Learning and onboarding. Cognitive load, scaffolding and time to first useful result.
  3. Weight and efficiency. Performance, accessibility and AI sizing.

15 days, three phases

Deliverables

  • Prioritized findings report
  • Redesigned flow
  • Clickable prototype
  • Implementation plan with metrics

What it isn’t: it doesn’t include back-end development or building a platform. I work on your flow and with your team, remotely. In Spanish; in English, in writing and asynchronously.
