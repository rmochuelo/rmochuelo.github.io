HOPE – Tejiendo Futuro

A EUR 1.1 million program designed in three weeks and approved by the European Union.

My role: I led the technical writing and methodology design, with Brot für die Welt, Fundación Mujer and Asociación Bosques Sin Fronteras.

The problem

Design the system, not just the document

Costa Rica hosts more than 500,000 Nicaraguans (UNHCR, 2024). Migrant women bear the brunt: barriers to employment, gender-based violence and discrimination.

The European Union opened a call that requires a logical framework and a verifiable source for every figure. The consortium decided to apply with about three weeks to go; consultants usually advise starting 3 to 6 months ahead.

A traditional approach couldn’t work. So my first design deliverable was the work system that would make the proposal possible.

The system

An agile team and a human + AI workflow

The team

Daily cycles: a stand-up to review status, a collaborative block from 7:00 to 15:00, then individual work. A Kanban board and a closing review to cut or redefine scope. My job: prioritize what carried the most weight in the evaluation and redefine scope every day.

The human + AI workflow

I defined the activities and tasks for both tracks, entrepreneurship and employability. AI helped me turn them into a logical framework in the format and language the EU requires. Indicators, figures and decisions were always validated by people. The accountant handled the budget, and Brot für die Welt the templates.

The data

I coordinated the research: every context figure came from UNHCR 2024, INEC, DGME or the CIEP-UCR survey. No figure went in without a source.

Decisions

Four decisions that shaped the design

  1. 01

    Cross-cutting inclusion instead of separate activities

    What we decided
    A minimum participation quota in every activity: 70% women, 20% young people, 5% people with disabilities and 5% LGBTIQ+, tied to the logical framework indicators.
    Why
    Dedicated LGBTIQ+ activities didn’t fit the budget or the timeline, and inclusion was a requirement of the call. Inclusion isn’t cut: it’s redesigned.

  2. 02

    A focused geographic scope

    What we decided
    School gardens (21 schools, 6,000 students) were concentrated in the districts of Upala and Los Chiles.
    Why
    That turned logistics from a risk into something we could budget.

  3. 03

    Remove the barrier to access

    What we decided
    Every training activity was budgeted with a per-participant transport allowance.
    Why
    For vulnerable migrant women, the cost of transport decides attendance. It’s the same principle I apply in digital products: remove the friction that stops people from getting there.

  4. 04

    A cross-cutting line with partners

    What we decided
    What didn’t fit in the consortium’s activities moved to the financial support to third parties line: five local organizations run, with funds tied to results, new-masculinities training with young people to prevent gender-based violence, school gardens, labor rights for Indigenous people, dialogue with Indigenous youth and community theater.
    Why
    Instead of cutting, we added partners with local experience. The large entrepreneurship fair also runs through this line.

The journey ends with a sale

An entrepreneur with a name, a brand and a product, but nowhere to sell, is only halfway there. Fundación Mujer runs institutional fairs and fairs inside large companies: the company opens its doors, its employees buy, and the employee association pays the entrepreneur on the spot. They last one to three days, weekly, every two weeks or monthly. Five groups from the Greater Metropolitan Area that finished the program already take part.

What I designed

What I designed

Planned in the approved proposal:

  • Two economic inclusion tracks: entrepreneurship (350 people) and employability (500), with seed capital, safe fairs and links to private companies.
  • 5 sales spaces (2 with private companies, 2 with local governments and a main fair at Casa del Cuño) and a job fair in the Greater Metropolitan Area.
  • Two AI courses: “Emprende IA – Conectadas al Futuro” (100 participants) and “AI for résumé writing” (120).
  • 6 trust and emotional support circles, plus community and leadership workshops against gender-based violence.
  • Awareness talks for 125 students at CTP de Upala and 300 young people in the Greater Metropolitan Area.
  • Methods: participatory popular education, “learning to be an entrepreneur”, human rights and intersectionality.

Brot für die Welt certifies that I led the strategic, conceptual and methodological development of all of Fundación Mujer’s activities.

Result

We delivered on time

The European Union approved the full proposal: 18 months, EUR 1.1 million, designed to reach 9,600 people. The program is underway, and I continue in it, teaching the branding and digital marketing workshops for the women entrepreneurs.

What I take away

  • With an impossible deadline, AI doesn’t replace the team. What works: clear priorities, verified data and AI for the heavy lifting.
  • The highest-impact design wasn’t an interface: it was the work system and the scope decisions.
  • Inclusion isn’t cut: it’s redesigned. And what doesn’t fit the budget is sustained with partners, until the entrepreneur gets to sell.
What’s behind the figures
  • EUR 1.1 M, 18 months, 9,600 people and the activities: proposal submitted to the NDICI – Global Europe call (annexes dated October 15, 2025). 9,600 is a target, not a result.
  • Role: letter from Brot für die Welt dated September 11, 2026.
  • Approval and execution: self-reported. The October 2025 thread with the EU Delegation shows the contracting phase.
  • Three weeks to build it and five groups at the fairs: self-reported.
  • 3 to 6 months: consultants’ advice for EU calls (GRANTfinder), not an NDICI rule.
  • No line-item amounts or participant data are published. The diagrams are illustrative reconstructions.
